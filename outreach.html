The strategy

Read first

For a chaotic co-op party game, coverage is not the goal — wishlists are. That reorders who you contact. A single mid-size streamer playing it "with the boys" out-converts a text article on IGN. Work the channels in this order, not alphabetically:

1

Creators & streamers

  • Highest wishlist conversion for co-op.
  • People wishlist a game they watched friends laugh at.
  • Mid-tier (10k–500k) reply more & convert better than mega-channels.
  • Send a free Steam key + a 20-second "why it's fun with friends" hook.
2

Steam curators

  • Recommend directly inside the store — closest to the wishlist button.
  • Reach them via Steam Curator Connect (send keys from Steamworks).
  • Co-op / party / local-multiplayer curators are the tight fit.
3

Press & trailer portals

  • Credibility, SEO, and a reason for creators to trust you.
  • Trailer aggregators (IGN/GameSpot trailers) = big reach, low effort.
  • Indie-focused & LATAM outlets reply far more than IGN's tips inbox.

The one rule for every message

Every email, DM, and pitch ends with one link and one ask: the Steam page, and "wishlist it / here's a key." Never bury it. Never send someone to a portal page when you want them on the Steam page.

Timing beats volume

Two moments move the wishlist needle most — plan the list around them:

  • Trailer / announcement drop — hit press + trailer portals the same day, embargo optional.
  • Demo live during Next Fest (19–26 OCT) — hit creators & curators hard; a playable demo is the single biggest wishlist driver. Line the list up 3–4 weeks before.

The impeccable press kit

What you send

A press kit is not an attachment — it's a single URL anyone can open, skim in 60 seconds, and pull assets from without emailing you back. Host it (presskit() / dopresskit.com, or a page on bananaairways.com/press). Below is the full spec; check each box before you send a single email.

📄 The essentials (must-have)

  • Steam page link — top of the page, impossible to miss wishlist target
  • Trailer — YouTube link + downloadable .mp4 must
  • One-line hook — "Chaotic co-op as crew on a doomed low-cost airline."
  • Short description — 2–3 sentences, the fantasy + the co-op chaos
  • Fact sheet — platform (PC/Steam), players (1–4 co-op), genre, release window, price, languages, dev name + location (Argentina)
  • Key art — Steam capsule + a clean logo (PNG, transparent)
  • Screenshots — 6–10, high-res, gameplay not menus must
  • GIFs — 3–5 short loops of the funniest chaos creators love these

✨ The credibility layer (sets you apart)

  • "For content creators" line — "Full monetization allowed, no embargo on the demo."
  • Steam key request — a form or clear "email us for a key" line
  • Feature list — 4–6 bullets (Serve · Repair · Defend · Fly)
  • Comparables — "For fans of Overcooked, Content Warning, PEAK, R.E.P.O."
  • Team / studio blurb — 3-person team, one paragraph, human
  • Logos & branding — zipped asset pack, download in one click
  • Contact — a real name + email that replies fast
  • Social links — Steam, X, Instagram, TikTok, Discord, YouTube

⚠ Three links you must slot in before sending anything

  • Steam page URL — (the wishlist target — the whole campaign depends on this being live)
  • Trailer URL — (YouTube, unlisted or public)
  • Hosted press kit URL — (presskit() page or bananaairways.com/press)

Every template below has these as [[PLACEHOLDERS]] — fill them once, reuse everywhere.

What to attach per channel (don't send the same thing to everyone)

  • Press / trailer portals: short pitch email + press kit link + trailer link. No key needed for trailer aggregators.
  • Creators / streamers: 2-line hook + Steam key (or key-request link) + 2–3 GIFs. Lead with "fun with friends," not features.
  • Steam curators: send the key via Curator Connect in Steamworks + a one-line why-it-fits note. Keep it short.

How to actually send it

Per channel

✉️ Press & trailer portals — by email/form

1
Find the tips/submission route

Use the email or form in the database. tips@ / news@ go to the whole desk — that's fine.

2
One outlet = one tailored subject line

"[Trailer] BANANA AIRWAYS — chaotic 4-player airline co-op (Steam, Next Fest)". Personalize the first line if you can.

3
Body: hook → 2 lines → links

Press-kit link + trailer + Steam page. Nothing attached; everything linked.

4
One follow-up, 5–7 days later

Short, polite, new angle (a fresh GIF or milestone). Never a third.

🎮 Creators — email first, DM second

1
Use the business email

YouTube "About" → "View email address". That inbox is for exactly this. Prefer it over DMs.

2
If no email, DM on their most-active platform

Usually X or Instagram (handle in the database). Keep it to 3 lines + key offer.

3
Lead with the key, not the ask

"Free key, full monetization, no embargo — want it?" Make saying yes frictionless.

4
Batch by tier

Send mid-tier first (they reply), use their coverage as proof when you pitch bigger channels.

🛒 Steam curators — Curator Connect

1
From Steamworks, open Curator Connect

Search the curator by name, send review keys directly — no email needed. Free and built for this.

2
Attach a one-line note

"Co-op chaos like Overcooked × Content Warning — thought it'd fit your list. Key attached, wishlist welcome."

3
Use the external contact only if listed

Some curators list an email/Discord/form on their page (in the database) — respect their stated preference.

4
Don't spam every curator

Steam limits Curator Connect volume. Prioritize genuine co-op/party fit or it looks like spam.

📸 Instagram / X DMs — when there's no email

1
Go to the exact handle in the database

Each creator/outlet row has the direct profile link. Open it, hit Message.

2
First line earns the second

No wall of text. "Hi [name] — made a co-op game I think your audience would lose it over. Free key?"

3
Drop one GIF, then the Steam link

A DM with a funny 3-second clip gets opened. A DM with a paragraph gets ignored.

4
Track who you hit

Mark the row in your CSV: contacted / replied / covered. Don't double-DM.
